# Progress

Phase-by-phase checklist for the Apex Auto demo site. See CLAUDE.md → "Apex Auto project brief" for the full plan and rules. One phase is built, reviewed, and committed at a time.

- [x] **Phase 0 — Setup**: Next.js + TypeScript project scaffolded; 3D and animation libraries installed (three, @react-three/fiber, @react-three/drei, @react-three/postprocessing, gsap, lenis).
- [x] **Phase 1 — Skeleton**: Fixed full-screen canvas with a placeholder cube; camera driven along a straight path by scroll progress via GSAP ScrollTrigger + Lenis; 600vh scroll track.
- [x] **Phase 2 — World**: Static procedural world — dark ground with a glowing teal grid, a boxy garage with an amber-lit open roller door, a teal neon "APEX AUTO" sign (Rajdhani Bold, SIL OFL), dusk fog/lighting, and a code-built hologram car inside. Camera path retargeted from outside the garage toward the door (choreography still comes in Phase 3).
- [x] **Phase 3 — Fly-through**: Scroll-driven "under the bonnet" camera sequence.
  - [x] 3A — Camera path, six named stages, HTML captions, stage indicator (1–6), and glowing marker dots on the car for stages 3–4. Camera follows a `CatmullRomCurve3` through hand-placed points in `scene/cameraPath.ts`, eased with `THREE.MathUtils.damp`; look-at target follows a second, gentler curve. `prefers-reduced-motion` disables the easing and cuts straight between stage shots. Rotated the hologram car 90° so it's nose-out toward the door (needed for the wheel/bonnet/side-view framing to make sense — noted here since it wasn't an explicit ask). Stage scroll ranges and captions live in `src/content/site.ts` as placeholder-flavoured UI copy, not business claims. Known tweak-later item: the Inspect→Wheels curve segment passes close to the right door pillar — worth an eyeball check once you can view it, may need an extra control point if it clips. Could not visually verify at 390px/1440px in-session (browser automation tool wasn't connected); layout uses responsive Tailwind sizing and should be checked by hand.
  - [x] 3B — Stage detail beats, all procedural, no model files:
    - **Wheels (stage 3)**: all four wheels (+ rims) now spin together, rotation tied directly to scroll progress (not a time loop) via `THREE.MathUtils.damp`-free direct assignment in `HologramCar.tsx`; a brake disc (spins with the wheel) and caliper (fixed) fade in/out on the focus front wheel in `BrakeAssembly.tsx`.
    - **Engine (stage 4)**: body/cabin dim to 40% opacity (`dimRange`) while a simple engine block (box + 2 intake cylinders + a pulley) fades in underneath in `EngineBlock.tsx`, with 3 floating HTML labels (drei `Html`) reading `siteContent.engineLabels` ("Oil", "Filter", "Battery").
    - **Diagnostics (stage 5)**: a thin teal scan plane sweeps front-to-back across the car in `Diagnostics.tsx`; wheels and cabin brighten once the scan passes their position (`HologramPart`'s `brightenAtX`, driven by `scan.ts`); 4 decorative readout chips (`siteContent.diagnosticsReadouts`) fade in beside the car — marked as fake/decorative in a code comment, per instructions.
    - Extracted the shared hologram-piece renderer into `HologramPart.tsx` (was inline in `HologramCar.tsx`) so the new brake/engine/diagnostics pieces could reuse it instead of duplicating fill+edge+fade boilerplate. Centralised the stage-fade math (`bandOpacity`/`hardBand`/`fadeOpacity`) into `stageFade.ts` and the `prefers-reduced-motion` check into `src/lib/useReducedMotion.ts`, refactoring `CameraRig.tsx` and `StageOverlay.tsx` to use them too instead of their own inline copies.
    - Reduced motion: wheel/disc spin freezes, the scan plane sweep is hidden, and all label/caption fades hard-cut instead of ramping. The scroll-position-driven "brighten once passed" effect is left on everywhere, since it's a direct function of scroll position, not a self-playing animation.
    - Performance: every new geometry is built once via `useMemo` and reused; no per-frame allocations (colours are lerped into a persistent `THREE.Color`, no `new Vector3()`/`new Color()` inside `useFrame`).
  - [x] 3C — Visual polish:
    - **Wireframe-to-solid reveal**: `HologramShell.tsx` replaces the body/cabin's plain fill with a small custom `ShaderMaterial` — edges stay the same glowing teal lines as every other part, but the faces fade from a faint translucent hologram to a dark solid fill as `uSolidify` (driven by scroll progress, updated only inside `useFrame`) goes 0→1, complete by the start of the diagnostics stage so the car reads as "fully built" by then.
    - **Post-processing**: `@react-three/postprocessing`'s `EffectComposer` with a subtle `Bloom` (so the sign, door glow and edges pick up a soft glow) and a light `Vignette`. A quality guard (`src/lib/deviceQuality.ts`) checks `navigator.hardwareConcurrency` and `devicePixelRatio`; on a low-end/mobile match, Bloom is skipped entirely (Vignette alone stays, it's cheap) and the canvas's `dpr` is capped at 1.5 instead of 2.
    - **Atmosphere**: `Atmospherics.tsx` adds ~260 slow-drifting dust points near the doorway (time-based drift, since it's ambient atmosphere rather than a scroll-tied effect like the wheels — frozen under `prefers-reduced-motion`) and a cheap additive-blended cone standing in for light spilling from the doorway onto the forecourt (no real light/shadow map).
    - **Loading screen**: `LoadingScreen.tsx` shows "APEX AUTO" and a thin bar, fading out once the scene renders its first frame (`SceneReady.tsx`, inside the Canvas). There's no meaningful byte-level progress to track (no large assets), so the bar jumps to full on ready rather than tracking a real percentage — noted here since it's not literally "progress".
    - **WebGL fallback**: `SceneErrorBoundary.tsx` (a class component, required for error boundaries) catches a failed Canvas mount and renders `FallbackSection.tsx` — business name, area, and a real Call button, all from `site.ts` — instead of the 3D scene.
    - Hit three of the project's stricter ESLint rules (`react-hooks/purity` / `react-hooks/immutability` / `react-hooks/refs`, part of the React Compiler ruleset) while building the dust particles and the shader uniforms: moved `Math.random()` to module scope instead of inside a hook, and made sure every per-frame mutation goes through a ref populated by React itself (JSX `ref={...}`) rather than closing over a `useMemo`/`useState` value or reading `.current` during render. Fixed, not suppressed.
    - Performance: I could not get a real browser connected this session (Claude-in-Chrome extension wasn't available) to measure actual frame rate — see my reply for a reasoned-but-unmeasured estimate instead of a fabricated number.
